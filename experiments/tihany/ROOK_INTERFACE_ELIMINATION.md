# Elimination of the residual degree-eight/degree-nine exceptional interface

## Scope and dependency

This note concerns the order-17 case. It starts from the finite local classification
certified independently in `DEGREE8_DEGREE9_INTERFACE.md`:

> If a degree-eight vertex `0` is adjacent to a degree-nine vertex `1`, their two
> completed neighbourhoods have one exceptional rooted 13-vertex interface, up to
> rooted isomorphism.

The steps below—from that unique interface onward—are independently reproduced by the
self-contained verifier `verify_rook_pattern_elimination.cpp`. They do not use the
SAT/CEGAR completion runs or floating-point optimization. The filename retains the
historical word `rook`, but the degree-nine neighbourhood is graph6 `HqolhhX`, not the
`3 x 3` rook graph.

We use the standard necessary properties of a hypothetical noncomplete double-critical
6-chromatic graph:

- `alpha(G)=4` in the order-17 reduction;
- `G` has no `K5`;
- `chi(G[N(v)]) <= 3` for every vertex `v`;
- every edge has at least four common neighbours;
- degree-eight vertices form an independent set;
- for every edge incident with either centre, the appropriate deletion colouring has
  all four colours on the common neighbourhood.

Let `R` be the fixed 13-vertex interface, with centres `0` and `1`. The only initially
unspecified edges of `R` are the twelve pairs between

```text
X={4,6,8},   Y={9,10,11,12}.
```

Let `O={13,14,15,16}` be the four vertices outside `R`. Since the centres have exact
degrees eight and nine, both are anticomplete to `O`.

## 1. Exact cross-edge census

The verifier enumerates all `2^12=4096` choices of the cross edges `X--Y`. For every
choice it checks, by exact backtracking:

1. the common-rainbow deletion colouring for every edge incident with `0` or `1`;
2. absence of a `K5` in the interface;
3. three-colourability of every interface neighbourhood.

Exactly sixteen cross masks survive:

```text
0 1 16 17 64 65 128 129 512 513 528 529 576 577 640 641
```

The bit order is

```text
(4,9),(4,10),(4,11),(4,12),
(6,9),(6,10),(6,11),(6,12),
(8,9),(8,10),(8,11),(8,12).
```

Equivalently, the surviving cross-edge sets are generated by three independent choices:

- optionally include `(4,9)`;
- include one of no edge, `(6,9)`, `(6,11)`, `(6,12)`;
- optionally include `(8,10)`.

## 2. Exact four-outside-vertex reduction

For a possible outside vertex `z`, write

```text
S_z = N_R(z) subseteq {2,...,12}.
```

A trace `S` is called locally admissible when both of the following necessary
conditions hold:

1. `R[S]` is three-colourable, because it is an induced subgraph of `G[N(z)]`;
2. for every `v in S`, the graph obtained from `R[N_R(v)]` by adjoining one vertex
   adjacent exactly to `S cap N_R(v)` is three-colourable, because it is an induced
   subgraph of `G[N(v)]`.

For each of the sixteen cross masks, exact enumeration gives

```text
locally admissible traces      1864
inclusion-maximal traces         13
```

Local admissibility is downward closed, so replacing any trace by an admissible
superset can only help the remaining degree and common-neighbour inequalities.
Consequently it is sufficient to consider the thirteen inclusion-maximal traces.

The verifier then enumerates every multiset of four maximal traces. It imposes exactly
the following necessary inequalities:

- every interface vertex has total degree at least eight;
- every interface edge has at least four common neighbours.

Only five cross masks admit four outside traces:

```text
0 16 17 64 128
```

Thus the earlier floating-point multicover computation has been replaced by a complete
integer enumeration of only `C(13+4-1,4)=1820` trace multisets for each cross mask.

## 3. Symbolic elimination of mask 0

In mask `0`, vertices `4,6,8` form a triangle and each has interface degree five. All
three are adjacent to the degree-eight centre `0`. Since degree-eight vertices are
independent, each of `4,6,8` has global degree at least nine. There are only four
outside vertices, so every member of `O` is adjacent to all three of `4,6,8`.

If two vertices of `O` were adjacent, those two vertices together with the triangle
`{4,6,8}` would form a `K5`. Hence `O` is independent. But `0` is anticomplete to
`O`, so `O union {0}` is an independent set of size five, contradicting
`alpha(G)=4`. Therefore mask `0` is impossible.

## 4. Symbolic elimination of masks 16, 17, 64 and 128

The same short colouring contradiction eliminates the four remaining masks. In each
of them:

- `8` has interface degree five;
- `4` has interface degree at most six;
- both `4` and `8` are adjacent to the degree-eight centre `0`;
- `5-12` is an edge and its common neighbours inside `R` are exactly `{1,2}`;
- the neighbourhood of `5` contains the three triangles

  ```text
  {0,4,8},  {0,1,2},  {1,2,12}.
  ```

Degree-eight independence implies `d_G(8)>=9` and `d_G(4)>=9`. Consequently `8` is
adjacent to all four vertices of `O`, while `4` is adjacent to at least three of them.

The edge `5-12` must have at least four common neighbours. Only `1` and `2` are
common neighbours inside `R`, so there are at least two outside common neighbours of
`5` and `12`. Since `4` misses at most one member of `O`, one of these outside common
neighbours, say `z`, is adjacent to `4`. It is also adjacent to `8`.

Take a proper three-colouring of `G[N(5)]`. The triangle `{0,4,8}` uses all three
colours, so `z`, adjacent to both `4` and `8`, must receive the colour of `0`. The
triangles `{0,1,2}` and `{1,2,12}` force `12` to receive that same colour. But
`z` is adjacent to `12`, a contradiction.

Therefore masks `16,17,64,128` are impossible.

## Consequence

Combining the independently certified 67-type interface classification with the exact
cross-mask reduction and the two symbolic contradictions proves:

> **Order-17 degree-eight/degree-nine exclusion.** An order-17 counterexample cannot
> contain an edge joining a degree-eight vertex to a degree-nine vertex.

Together with degree-eight independence:

> In an order-17 counterexample, every neighbour of a degree-eight vertex has degree
> at least ten.

The next target is to combine this high-neighbour condition with the two certified
degree-eight neighbourhood types and eliminate degree eight altogether in order 17.

## Verification boundary

Two separate C++ programs cover the finite portions:

1. `verify_degree8_degree9_interface.cpp` generates all 67 admissible degree-nine
   neighbourhoods, scans all 4656 compatible amalgams, and identifies the unique rooted
   13-vertex interface;
2. `verify_rook_pattern_elimination.cpp` starts from that interface, enumerates all
   4096 cross masks, all 2048 outside traces and all 1820 four-trace multisets, and
   checks every finite incidence used in the symbolic proof.

Neither program replaces the prose contradictions. The broader imported dependency is
the independently certified classification of the two possible degree-eight
neighbourhoods.
